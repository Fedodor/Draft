# Hybrid next-pick ranking — product-utility spike (2026-10-29, session 12)

Research only. No production inference change, no model export, no promotion, no
shipped-schema or threshold change, and the contaminated test partition was not
parsed or scored. Everything below is measured on the frozen validation partition,
which earlier sessions have already reused — treat all numbers as exploratory.

Question: **can the session-11 phase-aware ranking be shown honestly, and is it
stable enough to be a usable surface?**

Verdict up front: **the list is presentable and stable enough for a small private
usability test, but not accurate enough to be called advice.** Recommendation in
§6.

## 2. The prototype

Generated by [`ml/training/hybrid_presentation.py`](../ml/training/hybrid_presentation.py)
into the gitignored `ml/models/hybrid_spike/`:

| Artifact | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| `mockup.html` | self-contained page (no scripts, no network, no images); two runs produced a byte-identical file |
| `snapshots.json` | the 18 rendered states with the observed target kept out of the page (research data only) |
| `report.json` | fingerprints, routing, hit rates, stability, runtime, limitations |

The page shows six uniformly sampled validation drafts at picks 0, 2 and 5 — a
fixed seed, no accuracy-based selection. The sampled states' top entry matched the
observed pick in **2 of 18** cases, which is what a ~6% top-1 model should look
like; nothing was cherry-picked.

Per state it shows:

- the real interleaved draft prefix (picked and banned heroes, side, order);
- the acting side, how many legal heroes remain, and **which source** produced the
  list ("Observed pick frequencies (first two picks)" or "Contextual model (from
  the third pick on)");
- a five-hero ordered list with a qualitative tier bar;
- when the previous state's top entry is gone, an explicit note naming it.

What it deliberately does **not** show, unlike the existing
[`RecommendationCard`](../apps/desktop/src/components/RecommendationCard.tsx):
no score percentage, no confidence, no risk chip, no role labels, no hero
portraits implying strength, no "best pick" framing. The list is ordinal; the
bars are five fixed tiers; no probability is printed anywhere.

The claim rules are enforced in code, not just in review: the generator aborts if
the rendered document contains any of `best pick`, `win probability`, `win rate`,
`winrate`, `counter strength`, `optimal pick`, `% chance of winning`, or
`expected win`. The explaining copy therefore says "not a prediction of who wins"
rather than naming the thing it denies.

Cost, measured in this session:

| Quantity | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Model size (562 × 139 coefficients + intercept) | 0.56 MB |
| One draft state, one call (`predict_proba`) | **0.135 ms** |
| Batch throughput | 4.0 µs per state |
| Analysis peak (dominated by the 745,930-state training fit) | 1,765 MB |

Product implication: a ranking for a single state is a sub-millisecond CPU cost,
so the surface is cheap; it must still be computed off the UI thread and cached
per draft-state hash, exactly as the existing recommendation path already does. A
static mockup makes no claim about that plumbing.

## 3. Offline stability (validation)

Hit rates confirm the routing (top-1; 71,080 states from 7,108 drafts):

| | pick 0 | pick 1 | picks 2-7 | picks 8-9 | all |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| hybrid | 0.0990 | 0.0689 | 0.0490 | 0.0685 | 0.0599 |
| pooled model | 0.0744 | 0.0457 | 0.0490 | 0.0685 | 0.0551 |
| stage/side frequency | 0.0990 | 0.0689 | 0.0241 | 0.0461 | 0.0405 |

Adjacent-state churn (all 7,108 drafts per transition):

| Transition | Heroes removed | Top-1 removed by it | Any top-3 removed | Top-1 still first among survivors | Top-5 overlap | Kendall τ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pick 0 → 1 | 1.00 | 0.099 | 0.155 | 0.971 | 0.893 | 0.958 |
| pick 1 → 2 | 3.96 | 0.111 | 0.199 | 0.119 | 0.281 | 0.632 |
| pick 2 → 3 | 1.00 | 0.049 | 0.122 | 0.604 | 0.721 | 0.868 |
| pick 3 → 4 | 1.00 | 0.052 | 0.124 | 0.589 | 0.698 | 0.859 |
| pick 4 → 5 | 1.00 | 0.048 | 0.124 | 0.557 | 0.670 | 0.852 |
| pick 5 → 6 | 1.00 | 0.047 | 0.115 | 0.560 | 0.681 | 0.859 |
| pick 6 → 7 | 1.00 | 0.044 | 0.111 | 0.601 | 0.719 | 0.871 |
| pick 7 → 8 | 4.96 | 0.202 | 0.424 | 0.340 | 0.503 | 0.697 |
| pick 8 → 9 | 1.00 | 0.074 | 0.165 | 0.670 | 0.777 | 0.907 |

τ is computed on a fixed 2,000-draft sample (18,000 transitions) over the heroes
legal in both states; top-5 overlap is over all drafts.

Contextual list versus frequency list on the **same** state:

| Stage | Top-1 agreement | Top-5 overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| picks 0-1 | 1.000 | 1.000 (identical by routing) |
| picks 2-7 | 0.151 | 0.249 |
| picks 8-9 | 0.101 | 0.256 |
| all | 0.311 | 0.401 |

Kendall τ between the two lists: 0.689 on the sampled states.

### Reading it

- **Churn is driven by information, not noise.** Seven of the nine transitions
  remove exactly one hero, and there the top entry survives 56-60% of the time
  with τ ≈ 0.86: the list reorders mildly when one ban arrives. The two ban-heavy
  transitions (1→2 and 7→8, four to five removals) are where it really moves —
  1→2 also switches the source from frequency to contextual, which is the largest
  single change in the whole draft.
- **A stale list is the visible failure mode.** The top entry was removed by the
  immediately following action in 8.0% of transitions overall — but 20% at 7→8.
  A UI must therefore annotate removals instead of silently re-sorting; that is
  why the mockup names the disappeared top entry.
- **The contextual list is a different list, not a re-skin.** From pick 2 it
  disagrees with the frequency list at the top in ~85-90% of states and shares
  only a quarter of its top five. But top-1 accuracy of 4.9-6.9% over ~124 legal
  heroes means the argmax is weakly identified (the frequency list's own pick-2-7
  top-1 is 2.4%, barely above the ~0.8% chance level), so much of that
  disagreement is near-ties rather than a confident different opinion. Do not
  present the ordering as certainty at either end.
- **Stability is not quality.** A list that rarely moves can be consistently
  wrong; a list that moves may be reacting correctly to a ban.

Limitations: single model family and representation; validation reused by earlier
experiments; Kendall τ sampled (2,000 of 7,108 drafts) while the other columns use
all drafts; per-transition numbers are not bootstrapped, so treat small differences
between adjacent rows as noise.

## 4. Minimal user-feedback protocol

Goal: find out whether players **understand and would use** the list — not whether
it wins games.

**Participants.** 5-8 Captain's Mode players, ideally Ancient/Divine or above
(people who draft), recruited individually. ~30 minutes each, remote, screen
shared. Small on purpose: this is a comprehension test, not an experiment.

**Materials.** Three real drafts per participant, each shown at picks 2, 5 and 8.
Each state is shown twice, as "List 1" and "List 2", in randomised order and
without labels: one is the stage/side frequency list, the other the contextual
list. Picks 0-1 are excluded from the A/B because both sources are identical
there by design.

**Tasks.** Think-aloud while reading each list; then a short interview:

1. "What do you think this list is telling you?" (comprehension, unprompted)
2. "Would you consider any of these heroes here? Which, and why?" (use)
3. "How would you use it during a real draft, if at all?" (workflow)
4. Rate usefulness 1-5 and trust 1-5 per list.
5. Which of the two lists they would keep, and why.

**Comprehension check, asked last and scored separately.** "Does this list predict
which team wins?" and "Does the top hero mean it is the best pick?" Both must be
answered "no", with a correct restatement ("it shows what captains usually pick").
This is the criterion that decides whether the surface is honest in practice, and
it is not a preference question.

**Recorded data.** Anonymous session id, self-reported bracket, list version shown,
the two ratings, the preference, the comprehension answers, and short quotes. No
names, no account ids, no match history. Consent read at the start: purpose,
recording (audio only if they agree), right to stop, deletion after coding. Nothing
is published with attributable quotes.

**What this can and cannot show.** It can show whether the framing is understood,
whether the list is legible during a draft, and whether players see a use for it.
It cannot show any effect on win rate, draft quality, or pick value; those need a
separate randomised, adequately powered study with a pre-declared outcome.

**Proceed to a larger experiment only if all of these hold:**

- ≥ 80% of participants pass the comprehension check;
- median usefulness ≥ 3/5 and no one rates it useless (≤ 1);
- ≥ 60% say they would keep it open during a draft if it were opt-in;
- no participant, after debrief, describes the list as advice about what to pick
  or as a win predictor;
- at least half name a concrete use ("see what's likely banned next", "check my
  shortlist against common picks").

**Stop if**: comprehension fails, or players consistently read the top entry as
"the right pick", or usefulness clusters at 1-2. Do not "fix" a comprehension
failure with more copy on the same surface.

**Deliberately leading-question avoidance.** Participants are not told the list is
a model, not shown accuracy statistics, and not told which list is which until the
debrief. The two lists are visually identical.

## 5. Requirements for a fresh evaluation window

The current test partition (through `2026-08-28T07:44:36Z`) has already been
inspected and stays a regression reference only. A future claim needs:

1. **Data.** Matches with `start_time` strictly greater than
   `2026-08-28T07:44:36Z`, collected with the existing collector into a new raw
   directory (never merged into `ml/datasets/raw`). Keep the download immutable;
   record a per-file SHA-256 manifest and its fingerprint.
2. **Mode.** Derive game mode from the raw STRATZ `gameMode`, not the parquet's
   `game_mode` column (AUDIT P1-7). Freeze the mapping in the split config and
   record how many payloads fell outside it.
3. **Chronology.** Same rule as before: `order` dense and unique, unsupported
   payloads excluded and counted, never repaired. Record the exclusion table next
   to the split.
4. **Split.** Match-level, purely chronological: train / validation / holdout by
   `start_time` with explicit timestamp boundaries and counts in a new
   `ml/configs/eval_split_v3.json` that also pins the dataset SHA-256 and schema
   fingerprint. Fit and select on train/validation only; score the holdout once.
5. **Freeze before scoring.** Commit the split file, the dataset fingerprint, the
   feature/schema fingerprint, the routing constants and the metric definitions
   *before* any candidate touches the holdout. Any later change is a new window.
6. **Size.** Match the current window: ≥ 7,000 eligible Captains Mode drafts. With
   ~1,500 drafts only differences of roughly two top-1 points or more would be
   resolvable at p ≈ 0.06; if the window is smaller, publish it as directional
   only.
7. **Reporting.** One shot, full tables by stage, paired match-level bootstrap,
   and the constant baselines. The ML quality gate (ROC-AUC ≥ 0.60, Brier ≤ 0.235,
   ECE ≤ 0.10, ≥ 500 rows) applies to the win-probability target; it must not be
   repurposed as an imitation gate, and it must not be run against the
   contaminated window.

No gate run happened in this session, and the thresholds are untouched.

## 6. Recommendation

**Continue to a private usability prototype — with the §4 gate, not a production
build.**

Why:

- The list can be shown honestly: ordinal, sourced, legality-masked, with removals
  named instead of hidden, and with copy rules enforced by a test.
- Its behaviour is presentable: a single ban changes the order mildly (τ ≈ 0.86),
  and the large reshuffles coincide with the four-to-five-hero ban blocks and the
  source switch, which a UI can explain.
- The open question is comprehension and usefulness, which no offline metric can
  answer — and which a 5-8 player session answers cheaply.

What must not happen next: shipping it, calling it advice, presenting the ordering
as a probability, or claiming any win-rate effect. The accuracy (5.99% top-1,
13.1% top-3 overall) stays the headline limitation, so the framing is "what
captains commonly pick here", never "what you should pick".

If the usability gate fails, the correct outcome is to stop this line, not to add
features to the mockup.
